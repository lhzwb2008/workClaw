# Why Did Tech Drag the Market Lower Again?

## What Happened

**Session:** Tuesday, June 9, 2026 (latest full US close as of June 10)

- **S&P 500:** closed at **7,386.65**, down **19.08 points (-0.26%)** — fifth decline in six sessions; **-2.93%** over five days (MarketScreener, Pakistan Today)
- **Nasdaq Composite:** closed at **25,678.82**, down **250.84 points (-0.97%)** — **-5.22%** over five days; hit a **one-month intraday low** before recovering into the close (MarketScreener, DDS Finance)
- **Dow Jones Industrial Average:** closed at **50,872.11**, up **86.10 points (+0.17%)** — boosted by **Johnson & Johnson**, **Visa**, and **JPMorgan Chase** (Trading Economics)

**Sector moves:**

- **Information Technology** was the clear loser. The S&P 500 technology sub-index fell **1.8%** at the close after dropping more than **4%** intraday (Pakistan Today)
- **Semiconductors** led the decline. The Philadelphia Semiconductor Index ended **1.9% lower** after an **8.6%** intraday plunge (Pakistan Today). The iShares Semiconductor ETF fell **more than 3%**, erasing Monday's **6%** bounce (Trading Economics, Pakistan Today)
- **Notable chip decliners:** **Micron Technology** fell nearly **5%**; **Broadcom** slipped more than **2%** — both giving back Monday recovery gains after roughly **20%** two-day drops last week (Trading Economics, The Motley Fool)
- **Winners / rotation:** The Dow's gain reflected rotation into large-cap defensives and financials. **United Airlines** rose **3%** and **Delta Air Lines** **2.8%** as oil prices eased (Associated Press via ts2.tech)

**Other market signals:**

- **VIX:** spiked to an **intraday high of 23.34** during the midday selloff before easing (DDS Finance). The CBOE Volatility Index closed near **19.87** (Trading Economics)
- **Oil:** **Brent crude** settled at **$91.14**, down **3.3%** on the day (Associated Press via ts2.tech)
- **10-year Treasury yield:** slipped to **4.54%** from **4.56%** (ts2.tech) — a modest easing, but still elevated after Friday's hot jobs report
- **Breadth:** surprisingly positive beneath the surface — NYSE advancing stocks outnumbered decliners **1.33-to-1**; Nasdaq gainers held a slight edge (Pakistan Today). The headline indexes fell because a handful of massive tech weights dragged the cap-weighted benchmarks

**Context heading into Wednesday:**

- **May CPI** was scheduled for release **June 10 at 8:30 a.m. ET** — the last major inflation print before the **June 16–17** Federal Reserve meeting (BLS schedule, TradingKey)
- **SpaceX IPO** pricing expected around **June 11–12**, raising roughly **$75 billion** (Bloomberg) — a potential liquidity event markets were already positioning around

## Why It Moved

### 1. The chip trade ran out of bounce fuel

Think of last week's semiconductor rally like a rubber band stretched too far. After **Broadcom's** guidance disappointment triggered a **10%** sector plunge on **June 5**, Monday's **6%** chip rebound looked like a reset. Tuesday proved it was not. Micron and Broadcom sold off again, and the semiconductor ETF gave back the entire Monday recovery. When the sector that carried the market for two years hiccups twice in one week, the indexes follow — especially the Nasdaq, where tech is nearly half the weight.

### 2. Good news on jobs became bad news for growth stocks

Friday's **172,000** May payroll gain (BLS) was strong economic news — but on Wall Street, strong data means **higher-for-longer interest rates**. Traders pushed December Fed **hike odds toward roughly 68–70%** (Reuters, CNBC). That is like raising the interest rate on a credit card while you are still paying off an expensive purchase: the AI infrastructure bill keeps growing, but the financing cost just went up. Long-duration tech and chip stocks feel that first.

### 3. Geopolitics kept the inflation worry alive

President Trump said on social media that **Iran shot down a US Apache helicopter** near the Strait of Hormuz and vowed a response (Pakistan Today). That headline hit a market already nervous about energy-driven inflation ahead of CPI. Oil did fall **3.3%** on Tuesday, which helped airlines and eased one pressure point — but the geopolitical overhang reminded investors that energy prices can re-spike faster than a Fed dot plot can adjust.

- **May CPI (June 10, 8:30 a.m. ET):** Economists surveyed by Reuters expected headline inflation near **4.2% year over year** — an acceleration from April's **3.8%** (BLS). A hot print would reinforce rate-hike pricing and likely pressure tech; a soft print could reopen the door to a chip rebound. **Actual May CPI: data pending verification at time of publication.**

- **Fed meeting (June 16–17):** New Chair **Kevin Warsh's** first policy decision and press conference. Markets expect rates to hold at **3.50%–3.75%**, but the **dot plot** and statement language on inflation will matter more than the rate decision itself (CNBC, PrimeRates).

- **SpaceX listing and tech earnings:** The record IPO and upcoming reports from companies like **Oracle** could either drain more capital from crowded chip positions or restore confidence in the AI spending cycle — watch whether Tuesday's failed bounce becomes a third leg down or the floor.
