# Why Did the S&P 500 Slip After the Fed Held Rates Steady?

**One-sentence takeaway:** U.S. stocks fell more than 1% on June 17, 2026, not because the Fed raised rates — it didn't — but because updated projections flipped the year's script from "cuts possible" to "hikes on the table," lifting bond yields and pulling the rug from under long-duration growth stocks.

## What Happened

- **S&P 500:** Down **1.21%** (91.25 points) to **7,420.10**, per FRED and AP.
- **Nasdaq Composite:** Down **1.34%** (354.69 points) to **26,021.66**, the steepest loss among the three major indexes, per Yahoo Finance.
- **Dow Jones Industrial Average:** Down **0.98%** (507.12 points) to **51,492.55**, snapping a two-day streak of record closes, per AP.
- **Fed decision (2:00 p.m. EDT):** The FOMC voted **12–0** to hold the federal funds rate at **3.50% to 3.75%** — the fourth straight hold, per the official Fed statement.
- **Dot plot shift:** Among 18 policymakers who submitted forecasts, **nine** now see at least one rate hike by year-end; **eight** see no change; **one** sees a cut, per CNBC. The median end-2026 funds rate moved to **3.8%**, from **3.4%** in March, per the Fed's Summary of Economic Projections.
- **Inflation forecast raised:** Median 2026 headline PCE inflation revised to **3.6%**, from **2.7%** in March; core PCE to **3.3%**, per Fed.gov.
- **Treasury yields:** The **10-year yield** rose to **4.49%** from **4.43%**; the **2-year yield** jumped to **4.21%** from **4.05%**, per AP.
- **VIX:** Closed at **16.06**, down **2.13%** on the day, per Cboe — a modest move suggesting the selloff was more about repricing the rate path than panic.
- **Sectors:** **All 11 S&P 500 sectors closed lower.** Communication services was the worst performer, down roughly **2.9% to 3%**, per The Motley Fool and Reuters syndication. Industrials held up best, falling about **0.1%**. Consumer cyclicals dropped roughly **2.3%**.
- **Notable movers:** Mega-cap tech weighed on the index — Microsoft down **3.8%**, Amazon **3.5%**, Nvidia **1.3%**, Meta roughly **5.4%**, per AP and Motley Fool. SpaceX fell **4.9%**, its first loss since IPO. La-Z-Boy jumped **14.8%** on an earnings beat. ASML rose about **4%**, per Motley Fool.
- **Intraday reversal:** The Dow was up roughly **280 points** in the morning before the Fed release, then finished down **507 points**, per AP.
- **Oil:** Brent crude rose **0.7%** to **$79.55**, still well below wartime peaks above $100 but above pre-conflict levels near $70, per AP.
- **Retail sales:** May retail sales rose **0.9%** month over month, above the **0.5%** consensus, per Yahoo Finance — but gas-station sales surged **26.5%** year over year, reflecting higher fuel prices rather than pure demand strength.

## Why It Moved

**1. The Fed didn't hike — but it rewrote the forecast.**

Imagine your landlord saying the rent stays the same this month, then handing you a letter that next year's rent could go up. That is roughly what markets heard. Chair Kevin Warsh kept rates unchanged in his first meeting and even dropped forward guidance from the statement. But the dot plot — the quarterly grid of officials' rate expectations — erased the March signal for cuts and instead pointed toward a possible hike. Traders moved to price roughly an **84% probability** of at least one increase this year, up from **59.5%** the day before, per CME data cited by AP. Stocks sold off because the *path* changed, not because policy moved today.

**2. Higher yields pulled future profits back to earth.**

When bond yields rise, the math on long-duration assets gets harder. Tech and AI names — businesses whose biggest payoffs may be years away — feel this first. That is why the Nasdaq lagged less than the S&P 500 in percentage terms but still led the pain among major indexes. Communication services and consumer cyclicals — ad-driven, rate-sensitive corners of the market — fell hardest. Even "defensive" sectors like staples sold off because rising yields competed with their dividends. AP reported the 10-year yield's climb to 4.49%; every tick higher is another reason to discount tomorrow's earnings at a steeper rate today.

**3. A strong morning turned into a Fed afternoon.**

Before the 2:00 p.m. decision, stocks had been quietly optimistic — the Dow was green, oil was easing on U.S.–Iran deal hopes, and May retail sales beat expectations. The reversal after the projections release was swift: the S&P 500 gave up an earlier modest gain and closed near session lows at **7,402.61**, per Yahoo Finance historical data. Warsh himself told reporters he "didn't hear tons of conviction" behind the dots, but markets do not wait for conviction — they reprice probabilities immediately.

## What to Watch Next

- **June 18 triple witching session:** Quarterly options and futures expiration moves to **Thursday, June 18**, because markets close **Friday, June 19**, for Juneteenth, per NYSE calendars and MarketWatch. Expect heavier volume and choppier late-day trading as contracts roll — not necessarily a trend change, but a mechanical event that can amplify moves.
- **July–September inflation data:** The Fed raised its 2026 PCE forecast to 3.6% while oil was already falling toward **$79** a barrel. The next CPI and PCE prints will test whether sticky core inflation justifies the hike dots — or whether falling energy eventually pulls headline inflation down and softens the Fed's tone.
- **Warsh's communication reset:** The new chair omitted his dot, ended forward guidance, and announced task forces to review Fed operations. His next press appearances and any shifts in how the Fed talks about inflation will matter as much as the next rate decision itself.

The June 17 session was a reminder that in 2026, the Fed giveth with one hand (no hike today) and taketh with the other (a dot plot that says hikes may come). For short-form viewers, the story in one line: **rates didn't move, but expectations did — and the stock market noticed.**
