# Why Did the Dow Rise While the Nasdaq Fell on Monday?

Wall Street came back from a long weekend on Monday, June 22, 2026, and the session looked less like one unified verdict and more like two markets talking past each other. The blue-chip Dow Jones Industrial Average climbed on hopes that U.S.–Iran diplomacy could finally ease the energy shock that has fed inflation all spring. The technology-heavy Nasdaq Composite, meanwhile, slipped as elevated bond yields and worries about runaway artificial-intelligence spending kept pressure on the market’s most expensive names. The headline S&P 500 barely moved — a small gain that masked a sharp split beneath the surface.

The single story that best explains Monday is **geopolitical relief meeting macro anxiety**. Progress in Switzerland on a possible peace deal helped pull oil prices lower and gave cyclical, industrial America a lift. But the bond market did not celebrate with stocks: Treasury yields kept climbing, which acts like a higher discount rate on future tech profits. That is why memory-chip makers rallied hard while several giant cloud and social-media companies sold off on the same day.

## What happened (bullet facts with numbers)

- The **S&P 500** edged up **0.12%** to **7,509.28**, according to Google Finance, building on Thursday’s **7,500.58** close (CNBC/Yahoo Finance). The benchmark is coming off its **11th winning week in the last 12**, but remains roughly **1.3% to 1.7%** below its early-June record, per Associated Press reporting.
- The **Dow Jones Industrial Average** gained about **204 points**, or roughly **0.4%**, finishing near **51,769**, according to Invezz and AP session reporting. Industrial and financial heavyweights such as Caterpillar and Visa were among the index’s leaders in early trading, per Dow Jones headline summaries.
- The **Nasdaq Composite** fell **0.27%**, per Invezz, underperforming the other major benchmarks as large growth stocks faced higher borrowing-cost math.
- **Semiconductors and memory chips led the winners.** Micron Technology and Sandisk each rose about **5%**, and Intel climbed **4.8%**, according to Invezz, extending a year-long AI-hardware rally that has made memory one of the market’s hottest corners.
- **Large cloud and platform companies lagged.** Amazon, Oracle, and Meta each dropped more than **2%** amid concern that massive AI capital spending may take longer to convert into profits, according to Trading Economics session notes.
- **Energy-linked assets cooled as oil retreated.** Brent crude fell **2.8%** to **$78.29** a barrel, and U.S. West Texas Intermediate slipped **2.3%** to **$74.14**, per AP — well below the wartime peaks seen earlier this year but still above the roughly **$70** pre-war level cited by multiple outlets.
- **Sector performance was uneven.** Technology rose about **0.9%** and materials nearly **1%**, while communications services lagged, down about **0.4%**, according to Google Finance sector data for the session.
- **Deal-making moved individual names.** AbbVie agreed to acquire Apogee Therapeutics for roughly **$10.9 billion** in cash; Apogee shares surged about **46%** and AbbVie rose **4.3%**, per Invezz. SpaceX fell about **6%** after announcing a new bond sale, extending a pullback from its recent public-market debut.
- **Bond yields stayed firm.** The **10-year Treasury yield** climbed to **4.49%** from **4.46%** at Thursday’s close, per AP — and from just **3.97%** before the Iran war began — keeping financial conditions tighter than many equity investors would prefer.
- **Volatility ticked up slightly.** The Cboe Volatility Index, or **VIX**, was near **17.42**, up about **0.6%** on the day, according to Trading Economics — not panic levels, but enough to show the market is still weighing dual risks.
- **Markets were closed Friday, June 19**, for the **Juneteenth** federal holiday, so Monday’s session was the first chance to react to weekend diplomacy and to position ahead of key inflation data later in the week.
- **Alan Greenspan**, the former Federal Reserve chair who led the central bank from 1987 to 2006, **died at age 100** on Monday; tributes flooded financial media, though the news did not appear to drive broad index moves.

## Why it moved (3 short points, everyday analogies OK)

**1. Iran peace talk progress acted like a pressure release on the oil bill.**

For months, the war between the United States and Iran has kept energy traders on edge about shipments through the Strait of Hormuz — the narrow waterway that handles a large share of global oil traffic. Over the weekend and into Monday, negotiators in Switzerland described “encouraging progress” and outlined a **60-day roadmap** toward a final deal, with mediators from Qatar and Pakistan backing the tone. U.S. Vice President JD Vance said the talks created a “good foundation for a successful final deal.”

When the geopolitical temperature drops, oil often drops with it — like a restaurant finally getting a reliable food supplier again and taking the “shortage surcharge” off the menu. Lower oil helps consumers at the pump and eases one source of inflation, which tends to favor old-economy Dow components tied to travel, manufacturing, and heavy industry. That is the simplest read on why the Dow outran the Nasdaq on Monday.

**2. Lower oil did not translate into lower bond yields — and that kept tech on the back foot.**

You might expect falling energy prices to calm inflation fears across the board. But the bond market had a different reaction: yields rose anyway. Traders are still focused on whether the Federal Reserve may need to **raise interest rates later this year** to contain price pressures that built up during the conflict. AP cited market pricing implying nearly a **90% chance** of at least one Fed rate increase by year-end, up sharply from about **57%** a week earlier.

Higher yields are like raising the interest rate on a mortgage for the entire stock market’s future earnings. They hurt long-duration growth stocks the most — exactly the profile of many Nasdaq leaders. So even as chip makers cheered AI demand, several mega-cap platforms that are spending tens of billions on data centers sold off on fears that the bill is coming due before the revenue shows up. Monday’s split screen — Dow up, Nasdaq down — is what happens when geopolitics improves but monetary policy still looks restrictive.

**3. The AI trade is narrowing to “pick-and-shovel” winners while questioning the gold rush itself.**

Investors are treating the semiconductor supply chain as the clearest proof point that AI infrastructure spending is real and accelerating. Memory and chip-equipment names rallied sharply, with Micron’s upcoming Wednesday earnings seen as a pulse check on whether demand is still accelerating after a nearly **300%** year-to-date run, per multiple outlets.

At the same time, the companies funding that build-out — the hyperscale cloud and platform giants — faced skepticism about whether returns on AI investment will arrive fast enough to justify today’s spending wave, estimated at more than **$700 billion** industry-wide this year. Think of it as a gold-rush town where shovel sellers are thriving while the mine owners worry about how much ore is actually left. That rotation — chips up, big spenders down — defined Monday’s internal market story far more than the S&P 500’s tiny headline move.

## What to watch next (2-3 objective bullets, not trade advice)

- **Thursday’s May Personal Consumption Expenditures price index** is the week’s main macro event. Economists expect inflation to remain elevated — AP cited expectations for the headline measure near **4.1%** in May versus **3.8%** in April — and any surprise could shift Fed rate-hike expectations that already moved higher after last week’s hawkish central-bank meeting under Chair Kevin Warsh.
- **Wednesday’s Micron earnings report** will test whether the AI memory boom still has fundamental support or is running on momentum alone. Strong guidance could keep the chip complex leading; a cautious outlook may ripple through the entire technology leadership group.
- **The next chapter of U.S.–Iran diplomacy** remains a live variable for oil, inflation, and global risk appetite. A concrete deal within the stated **60-day window** could further ease energy costs; renewed threats around the Strait of Hormuz or Lebanon could quickly reverse Monday’s oil relief.

Monday did not rewrite the market’s year-long story — it sharpened it. Geopolitics offered a modest tailwind, but the Fed and the AI spending cycle still decide who wins and who loses on any given day. Until inflation data confirm that price pressures are truly cooling, expect more sessions where the Dow and the Nasdaq tell different versions of the same economy.
